# Scientific Observation Instrumentation Form

## Summary of the Paper
- **Content Summary**: This paper investigates the structure of solar coronal mass ejections (CMEs) in both the extreme-ultraviolet (EUV) and white-light passbands, with a focus on the early eruption stage in the low corona (below 1.3 R⊙). The study employs multiple instruments to observe different aspects of CME morphology – notably the three-part structure (leading edge, low-density zone, and core which may consist of a filament or hot channel). EUV data are provided by instruments aboard SDO, STEREO, and GOES-16, while white-light coronagraphs aboard SOHO and STEREO are used to examine the high-corona evolution. Specific observation times are associated with four limb events (occurring on 2013 September 24, 2015 February 9, 2014 January 06, and 2017 September 10) which are used to correlate the low-corona EUV appearances with the white-light coronagraph images.

## Instrumentation Details

### 1. Atmospheric Imaging Assembly (AIA) on board the Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO)
- **General Comments**:
   - AIA images the low corona with a field of view (FOV) of 1.3 R⊙, a high cadence of 12 s, and a resolution of 0.6″ per pixel. It provides observations in seven EUV passbands. In this study AIA data at 193 Å and 131 Å are used to reveal the early three-part appearance of CMEs (i.e., the leading edge, filament, or hot channel, and the intervening low-density zone).
- **Supporting Quote**: 
   "The AIA records the low corona with the FOV (ﬁeld of view) of 1.3 R⊙, cadence of 12 s and resolution of 0.′′6 per pixel in seven EUV passbands."

### 2. Extreme Ultraviolet Imager (EUVI) on board the Solar Terrestrial Relations Observatory (STEREO)
- **General Comments**:
   - EUVI provides solar EUV images at four wavelengths with a field of view of 1.7 R⊙. It partially overlaps with the white-light coronagraph COR1 (FOV of 1.4–4 R⊙) such that continuous observations from the solar surface to the outer corona are possible. Both STEREO-A and STEREO-B are used to capture events from distinct viewing angles.
- **Supporting Quote**:
   "The EUVI provides the solar EUV images at four wavelength with an FOV of 1.7 R⊙, partially overlapping with that of COR1 (1.4–4 R⊙)..."

- **General Comments**:

- **Supporting Quote**:
   "The SUVI images the solar corona with an FOV larger than 1.6 R⊙ in the horizontal direction in six EUV passbands."

### 4. COR1 Coronagraph on board STEREO
- **General Comments**:
   - COR1 is a white-light coronagraph with a field of view of approximately 1.4–4 R⊙. It detects Thomson-scattered light from free electrons, thereby revealing density structures in the corona. It is used in conjunction with EUVI to link observations from the inner corona to the white-light features.
- **Supporting Quote**:
   "The white-light coronagraphs COR1 (Howard et al. 2008) on board the STEREO... are used to analyze the CME structure in the high corona."

#### Data Collection Period 1: 2013 September 24 (STEREO-B)
- **Time Range**: Observations at 20:16 UT and twenty minutes later (circa 20:36 UT) on 2013 September 24  
   - **Supporting Quote**: 
      "At 20:16 UT, the ﬁlament as denoted with the red dots is still blocked by the occulter... Twenty minutes later, the ﬁlament ascends as presented in Figure 2(c) and (d)..."
- **Wavelength(s)**: White-light (imaging Thomson-scattered light from free electrons)
   - **Supporting Quote**: 
      "...the dots are superimposed on the COR1 image recorded at the same time, which clearly demonstrates that the ﬁlament corresponds to the CME core."
- **Physical Observable**: Changes in the density structure as the low-corona features (filament/leading edge) evolve into the bright front and core in white-light.
   - **Supporting Quote**: 
      "Therefore, we conclude that the leading edge and ﬁlament observed in the EUV passband evolve into the bright front and core of three-part CME in the white-light images."

#### Data Collection Period 2: 2017 September 10 (STEREO-A)
- **Time Range**: Starting observations from 16:05 UT on 2017 September 10  
   - **Supporting Quote**: 
      "The running-diﬀerence image of COR1 A... after they entered into the COR1 FOV at 16:05 UT."

   - **Supporting Quote**:

- **Physical Observable**: Imaging of the evolving CME structure – particularly the bright front (leading edge) and the developing core as tracked from the low to higher corona.
   - **Supporting Quote**: 
      "...demonstrates again that the leading edge and the hot blob evolve into the bright front and the core of CME, respectively."
