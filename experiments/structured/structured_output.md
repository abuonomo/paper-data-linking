# Scientific Observation Instrumentation Form

## Summary of the Paper

**Content Summary:** This study analyzes current sheet candidates observed in the aftermath of CMEs using both white light and ultraviolet observations. The primary instruments employed are the LASCO C2 white light coronagraph and the UVCS ultraviolet spectrometer, both onboard the SOHO spacecraft. LASCO C2 images, collected during two main periods (1996 April–1998 December for solar minimum and 2001 for solar maximum), were used to detect ray-like features. UVCS observations, spanning from 1996 June to 2005 December, provided spectral data in key lines (e.g., O VI, H I Lyα, Si XII, and [Fe XVIII]) to assess plasma properties. Several other instruments—including SUMER, the Transition Region and Coronal Explorer, the Solar Dynamics Observatory/Atmospheric Imaging Assembly (AIA), STEREO coronagraphs, and the MLSO Mark-III/Mark-IV K-coronameters—are referenced in the text to provide additional context regarding observational techniques and supporting evidence from different wavelengths.

## Instrumentation Details

### 1. LASCO C2 on board SOHO (PRIMARY)

**General Comments:**
> LASCO C2 is a white light coronagraph that continuously observes the solar corona from 2.3 to 6 R⊙ with a cadence of about 20 minutes and a pixel size of 11.4 arcseconds. It is used to detect ray-like features associated with current sheets in the aftermath of CMEs.

**Supporting Quote:**
> LASCO C2 is a white light coronagraph, that continuously observes the solar corona from 2.3 to 6 R⊙ with a cadence of about 20 min and a pixel size of 11.4′′ (Brueckner et al. 1995).

#### Data Collection Period 1: White light observations during solar minimum.

- **Time Range:** 1996 April – 1998 December
  - *Supporting Quote:* The white light selected rays cover the periods 1996 April through 1998 December to sample solar minimum and the year 2001 to sample solar maximum.

- **Wavelength(s):** White light (broadband visible)
  - *Supporting Quote:* LASCO images are white light observations that reveal the ray‐like features in the wake of CMEs.

- **Physical Observable:** Brightness and morphological features of current sheet candidate rays in the corona.
  - *Supporting Quote:* The white light ray-like features we analyzed in this paper were selected from the LASCO C2 observations.

- **Additional Comments:** Used to identify and track the evolution of ray-like features behind CMEs.

#### Data Collection Period 2: White light observations during solar maximum.

- **Time Range:** 2001 – 2001
  - *Supporting Quote:* The white light selected rays cover the periods 1996 April through 1998 December to sample solar minimum and the year 2001 to sample solar maximum.

- **Wavelength(s):** White light (broadband visible)
  - *Supporting Quote:* LASCO images provide a complete view of the solar corona in white light.

- **Physical Observable:** Ray-like structures and brightness enhancements indicating current sheet morphology.
  - *Supporting Quote:* The images show the pre-CME corona, the ray in the wake of the CME, and the post-ray corona.

- **Additional Comments:** Serves as the primary dataset for selecting candidate current sheets during solar maximum.

### 2. UVCS (UltraViolet Coronagraph Spectrometer) on board SOHO (PRIMARY)

**General Comments:**
> UVCS is a spectrometer with a narrow entrance slit that can be positioned at various polar angles and heliocentric heights (1.5 to 9 R⊙). It observes key ultraviolet emission lines, including the O VI doublet, H I Lyα, Si XII, and the high-temperature [Fe XVIII] line, to diagnose the plasma conditions in the corona.

**Supporting Quote:**
> Unlike the WL coronagraph with its complete view of the solar corona, the UVCS spectrometer has a field of view defined by its narrow entrance slit 42′ long and up to 82′′ wide. The slit can be placed at any polar angle and at heliocentric heights between 1.5 and 9 R⊙. UVCS has two UV channels optimized to detect the O VI λλ 1032, 1037 doublet and HI Lyα lines.

- **Detectors:** Two UV channels with spatial pixels of 7 arcseconds and spectral pixels of 0.0993 Å (OVI channel) and 0.1437 Å (Lyα channel)
  - *Supporting Quote:* The spatial pixels are 7′′ and spectral pixels are 0.0993 ˚A (0.0915 ˚A for the redundant path) and 0.1437˚A for the OVI and LYA channels, respectively.

#### Data Collection Period 1: UV spectroscopic observations covering current sheet features.

- **Time Range:** 1996 June – 2005 December
  - *Supporting Quote:* The UV selected rays were obtained from the period 1996 June through 2005 December.

- **Wavelength(s):** 945 to 1270 Å (first order), 473 to 635 Å (second order), [Fe XVIII], Si XII, O VI, H I Lyα, C III L977
  - *Supporting Quote:* The spectra always included the O VI doublet, Si XII λλ 499 or 521, Lyα, Lyβ and C III L977. During most of 2001, the synoptic scans also included the high temperature line of [Fe XVIII].

- **Physical Observable:** Ultraviolet emission lines diagnostic of plasma temperature, density, and dynamics in current sheets.
  - *Supporting Quote:* In ultraviolet spectra, current sheets were identified as narrow regions of very hot plasma seen with UVCS between flare loops and CME cores.

- **Additional Comments:** Data were acquired both from daily synoptic scans and targeted observations with varying spatial and spectral resolutions.

### 7. MLSO Mark-III/Mark-IV K-Coronameters on board Mauna Loa Solar Observatory (MLSO) (CONTEXTUAL)

**General Comments:**
> The MLSO K-coronameters are ground-based instruments that produce polarization brightness maps of the solar corona and are used when the UVCS slit is positioned below the LASCO C2 occulter.

**Supporting Quote:**
> For those events in which the UVCS slit was located below the LASCO C2 occulter disk we also examined, whenever the data were available, the Mark-III or Mark-IV K-coronameter data from Mauna Loa Solar Observatory (MLSO). The MLSO coronagraphs are ground-based instruments that produce polarization brightness maps of the solar corona from 1.14 to 2.86 R⊙ in the range 7000 - 9000 ˚A with a 3 min cadence.

- **Detectors:** Polarimetric imaging detectors
#### Data Collection Period 1: Routine ground-based observations.

- **Time Range:** Approximately 17:00 UT – Approximately 02:00 UT
  - *Supporting Quote:* The typical observing period is from ∼17:00 to ∼02:00 UT each day.

- **Wavelength(s):** 7000 - 9000 Å
  - *Supporting Quote:* MLSO coronagraphs produce polarization brightness maps of the solar corona in the range 7000 - 9000 ˚A with a 3 min cadence.

- **Physical Observable:** Polarization brightness maps of the corona.
  - *Supporting Quote:* The MLSO coronagraphs are ground-based instruments that produce polarization brightness maps of the solar corona from 1.14 to 2.86 R⊙.

- **Additional Comments:** Used for supplementary comparison when UVCS observations are taken below the LASCO occulter.
