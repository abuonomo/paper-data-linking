# Scientific Observation Instrumentation Form

## Summary of the Paper
- Content Summary: The paper analyzes apparent elongation motion of flare ribbons along polarity inversion lines (PIL) and the shear of flare loops in multiple two-ribbon flares, quantifying speeds and loop inclinations to infer properties of coronal reconnection. Primary data sources are SDO/AIA UV 1600 Å and EUV passbands (131, 171, 94, 335) for ribbon and loop evolution, SDO/HMI line-of-sight photospheric magnetograms for PIL mapping and magnetic context, TRACE 1600 Å for earlier events, SOHO/MDI magnetograms for TRACE-era magnetic context, and RHESSI hard X-ray data for timing relative to ribbon evolution. Key observation periods include SOL2011-09-13T22:00 (AIA/HMI) with elongation lasting nearly an hour, SOL2011-12-26T11:23 (AIA/HMI) with ~10 minutes of rapid elongation, TRACE-era events such as segments on 2000-07-04 from 10:20:44–10:22:59 UT and 10:24:10–10:26:56 UT, and 2005-01-15 from 22:41:52–22:45:07 UT, as well as RHESSI timing for 2005-05-13 with a 16:43 UT HXR peak. These observations underpin measurements of ribbon elongation speeds (few to ~100 km s−1), loop-spreading speeds across EUV temperatures, and evolving loop/footpoint inclination (shear).

## Instrumentation Details

### Atmospheric Imaging Assembly (AIA) on board Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO)
- General Comments:
  - AIA UV 1600 Å images were used to identify and track newly brightened flare ribbon fronts and measure elongation and perpendicular motions. AIA EUV passbands (131, 171, 94, 335 Å) were used to track flare loop formation and spreading at different plasma temperatures and to quantify loop-top motion and shear evolution.
- Supporting Quote: "The first two flares were observed by AIA, and the last by TRACE."

#### Data Collection Period 1: SOL2011-09-13 flare ribbon elongation and loop spreading
- Time Range: 2011-09-13T22:00 – approximately 2011-09-13T23:00 UT
  - Supporting Quote: "The C-class flare SOL2011-09-13T22:00 exhibits persistent elongation motion of the positive ribbon front, with little perpendicular expansion following the elongation."
  - Supporting Quote: "Elongation of the ribbon in the plage of the positive magnetic field proceeds for nearly an hour."
- Wavelength(s): UV 1600 Å; EUV 131 Å, 94 Å, 335 Å, 171 Å
  - Supporting Quote: "These flares exhibit two ribbons well-observed in the UV 1600 Å passband on both sides of the PIL"
  - Supporting Quote: "In four passbands 131 (10 MK), 94 (6 MK), 335 (3 MK), and 171 (1 MK), measurements tracking the front yield a mean speed of 13.9 ± 0.7, 11.2 ± 1.6, 9.8 ± 1.8, 9.6 ± 0.5 km s−1, respectively."
- Physical Observable: Flare ribbon brightening and subsequent flare loop formation/spreading along the PIL
  - Supporting Quote: "Flare ribbons and loops spread along the PIL at a speed ranging from a few to a hundred km s−1."
  - Supporting Quote: "The ribbon brightening is then followed by sequentially formed flare loops first showing up in the northeast, and then spreading down along the PIL."
- Additional Comments: Pre-flare loop context from 335 Å
  - Supporting Quote: "Pre-flare loops are visible in the 335 Å passband, suggesting that the typical temperature of the pre-flare arcade is about 3 MK. A set of loops is selected manually along the axis of the arcade observed in a 335 Å image taken at 21:00 UT, about 1.5 hours before the flare onset."

#### Data Collection Period 2: SOL2011-12-26 flare ribbon and loop spreading
- Time Range: 2011-12-26T11:23 – approximately 2011-12-26T11:33 UT
  - Supporting Quote: "The two-ribbon flare SOL2011-12-26T11:23 exhibits two nearly symmetric flare ribbons, both of which start to brighten from the southwest end of the AR and spread along the PIL toward the northeast."

### Helioseismic and Magnetic Imager (HMI) on board Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO)
- General Comments:
  - HMI line-of-sight (longitudinal) magnetograms were used to outline the PIL, provide photospheric magnetic context, and derive current direction used to classify elongation directionality relative to current.
- Supporting Quote: "In practice, it has been assumed that ⃗Bn does not change significantly from the photosphere to the chromosphere, nor during the flare, so that ⃗Bn is usually taken from conventional photospheric magnetograms such as those provided by MDI and HMI."
