# Scientific Observation Instrumentation Form

## Summary of the Paper
- **Content Summary**: The study analyzes a complex solar event on 2005-01-17 involving two fast CMEs that interacted in interplanetary space and multiple radio burst episodes (type III, type II, and type IV). Primary data sources include ground-based metric radio spectrography (ARTEMIS-IV), space-based low-frequency radio observations (Wind/WAVES and its TNR receiver), SOHO/LASCO coronagraph imaging, GOES soft X-ray fluxes, RSTN 15.4 GHz microwave fluxes, and in-situ energetic electron measurements by ACE/EPAM (with ACE/SWEPAM solar wind speeds). The results hinge on timing and spectral diagnostics across 650 MHz–20 kHz radio bands, CME kinematics from LASCO, SXR/microwave profiles for flare staging, and near-relativistic electron intensities and anisotropies at 1 AU. Key observation periods include flare stages and type III groups (09:07–09:28 UT, 09:43–09:59 UT), CME signatures (LASCO detections at 09:30 and 09:54 UT, heights at 11:30 UT), a distinct low-frequency type III group near 11:30 UT associated with CME interaction, and ACE/EPAM electron rises at ~10:00 UT and ~12:00 UT.

## Instrumentation Details

### ARTEMIS-IV solar radio-spectrograph on board [Facility: ARTEMIS-IV]
- **General Comments**:
  - ARTEMIS-IV provided dynamic spectra in the metric range to identify and analyze type III bursts (III1, III2), type II emission, and an extended type IV continuum with fine structures, including high-resolution observations revealing pulsations, fiber bursts, and zebra patterns.
- **Supporting Quote**: "There we present the dynamic flux density spectrum of the radio bursts in the 650 MHz-20 kHz range (heliocentric distance ≈1.1 R⊙to 1 AU) using combined recordings of the Appareil de Routine pour le Traitement et l’Enregistrement Magn´etique de l’Information Spectrale (ARTEMIS-IV) solar radio-spectrograph (Caroubalos et al., 2001; Kontogeorgos et al., 2006) and the Wind/WAVES experiment (Bougeret et al., 1995)."

#### Data Collection Period 1: Type III1 metric counterpart and fine structures
- **Time Range**: 2005-01-17 09:07 – 09:28 UT
  - **Supporting Quote**: "Type III1        09:07-09:28   0.2-630 MHz          AR10720"
- **Wavelength(s)**: 200–500 MHz; 290–415 MHz; up to 630 MHz for combined context
  - **Supporting Quote**: "From the high-resolution observations in the 200- 500 MHz range (see figures 5, 6 for example)  it appears that most bursts are broadband pulsations."
- **Physical Observable**: Type III bursts with high-frequency fine structures (pulsations, RS and ordinary-drift bursts, fiber bursts) superposed on type IV continuum
  - **Supporting Quote**: "Among these fine structures were broadband pulsations, bursts with ordinary and reverse drift, and fiber bursts due to whistlers travelling upwards in the corona (see Figure 5, e.g., 09:16:20-09:16:45 UT)."
- **Additional Comments**: ARTEMIS-IV captured the evolving spectral character across the CME1 track, differentiating low- and high-frequency behaviors during III1.

#### Data Collection Period 2: Type III2 metric counterpart and fine structures
- **Time Range**: 2005-01-17 09:43 – 09:59 UT
  - **Supporting Quote**: "Type III2        09:43-09:59   0.2-630              AR10720"
- **Wavelength(s)**: 300–400 MHz; up to 630 MHz for combined context
  - **Supporting Quote**: "The high-resolution spectrogram in the 300-400 MHz range (Fig. 6) shows a wealth of individual bursts with different drift rates and zebra pattern."
- **Physical Observable**: Type III bursts with mixed drift signatures; zebra pattern; activity indicating acceleration below CME front and onset of DH type III bursts
  - **Supporting Quote**: "A close look at the dynamic spectrum (bottom panel of Figure 4) reveals negative overall drifts below 100 MHz, while burst groups with positive overall drift prevailed above 130 MHz."
- **Additional Comments**: ARTEMIS-IV documented the spectral complexity during stage 2, complementing DH observations.

#### Data Collection Period 3: Type IV continuum
- **Time Range**: 2005-01-17 08:40 – 15:24 UT
  - **Supporting Quote**: "The type IV continuum started near 08:40 UT during the initial smooth increase of the SXR flux before stage 1." 
- **Wavelength(s)**: Metric range extending down to 5 MHz
  - **Supporting Quote**: "It dominated the metre wave spectrum during and after Type III2, and gradually penetrated to lower frequencies, down to 5 MHz."
- **Physical Observable**: Broadband type IV continuum with fine structures (pulsations, spikes, fiber bursts, zebra patterns)
  - **Supporting Quote**: "From the high-resolution observations in the 200- 500 MHz range (see figures 5, 6 for example)  it appears that most bursts are broadband pulsations."
- **Additional Comments**: Type IV persisted throughout both flare stages, indicating ongoing coronal acceleration.

### Wind/WAVES (including TNR) on board Wind
- **General Comments**:
  - Wind/WAVES provided decametric–kilometric dynamic spectra, identifying two major type III groups (III1, III2), the low-frequency type III3 near CME interaction, merged/continuing type II emission, Langmuir wave packets near the local plasma frequency, and time–frequency drift diagnostics.
- **Supporting Quote**: "The dominant features in the dynamic spectrum observed by Wind/WAVES at frequencies below 2 MHz are two groups of type III bursts, labelled III1 and III2."

#### Data Collection Period 1: Low-frequency signatures during flare stages (III1 and III2)
- **Time Range**: 2005-01-17 09:07 – 09:59 UT
  - **Supporting Quote**: "The dominant features in the dynamic spectrum observed by Wind/WAVES at frequencies below 2 MHz are two groups of type III bursts, labelled III1 and III2."
- **Wavelength(s)**: <2 MHz
  - **Supporting Quote**: "observed by Wind/WAVES at frequencies below 2 MHz"
- **Physical Observable**: DH–km type III groups associated with stage 1 and stage 2 flare energy release
  - **Supporting Quote**: "They occurred in association with the SXR and microwave emissions of stages 1 and 2, respectively, and with the two different parts of the Hα flare."
- **Additional Comments**: These emissions align with extrapolated CME liftoff times.

#### Data Collection Period 2: CME interaction-associated low-frequency type III (III3)
- **Time Range**: 2005-01-17 11:28 – 11:40 UT
  - **Supporting Quote**: "The spectrum shows a chain of narrow-band emissions with negative frequency drift, indicating the type II bursts, followed by the high-frequency part of type III3 between 11:28 and 11:40 UT."
- **Wavelength(s)**: Starting frequency 0.6 MHz; TNR analysis 35–256 kHz; local fpe ~35 kHz; Langmuir packets near 32 kHz
  - **Supporting Quote**: "Well after the decay of the SXR and microwave emission a third group of bursts (III3 in Figure 3) is identified (near 11:30 UT), with unusually low starting frequency (0.6 MHz)" 
  - **Supporting Quote**: "The narrow-band short bursts near 32 kHz are Langmuir wave packets."
  - **Supporting Quote**: "The open triangles show the time of maximum of the burst at each frequency in the range 35-256 kHz."
- **Physical Observable**: Short, fast-drifting type III bursts indicating rapid electron escape; type II lane continuation/merging; local plasma frequency evolution
  - **Supporting Quote**: "The time profiles show that the peak times are not distinguishable over a large part of the frequency spectrum with 1-min integrated data, but that the centre of gravity of the brightest feature shifts to later times at the lower frequencies."
- **Additional Comments**: TNR enabled precise timing and drift analysis; emission linked to CME interaction altitudes.

### SOHO/LASCO Coronagraph on board SOHO
- **General Comments**:
  - LASCO white-light coronagraphy provided CME detection, sky-plane speeds, liftoff chronology, and CME front heliocentric distances vital to interaction timing and comparison with radio starting frequencies.
- **Supporting Quote**: "Two CMEs were observed in close succession. A sequence of difference images from the Large Angle and Spectrometric Coronagraph (LASCO) aboard the SOHO spacecraft (Brueckner et al., 1995) is displayed in Figure 2"

#### Data Collection Period 1: CME detections and speeds
- **Time Range**: 2005-01-17 09:30 – 09:54 UT
  - **Supporting Quote**: "the first CME (henceforth CME1) is seen in the image at 09:30 UT in the north-western quadrant. While it travelled through the corona, the second, broader CME (CME2) appeared underneath (image at 09:54 UT)."
- **Wavelength(s)**: Not applicable (white-light coronagraphy)
- **Physical Observable**: CME plane-of-sky speeds; halo CME classification
  - **Supporting Quote**: "Speeds of, respectively, 2094 and 2547 km s−1 were derived from linear fits to the trajectories of their fronts published in the CME catalogue."
- **Additional Comments**: These kinematics anchor the radio–CME association and interaction timing.

#### Data Collection Period 2: CME front heights and formal intersection
- **Time Range**: 2005-01-17 11:30 – 12:32 UT
  - **Supporting Quote**: "From the LASCO observations and the uncertainties resulting from the straight-line fits to the CME front trajectories, the heliocentric distances of the CME fronts at 11:30 UT are, respectively, (26.9 ± 4.8) R⊙and (24.5 ± 5.5) R⊙."
  - **Supporting Quote**: "Formally the CME fronts described by the fits intersected near 12:32 UT at a heliocentric distance of about 38 R⊙."
- **Wavelength(s)**: Not applicable
- **Physical Observable**: CME interaction geometry consistent with radio starting frequency heights
  - **Supporting Quote**: "The burst group III3 is hence consistent with harmonic emission from the vicinity of the CME fronts."
- **Additional Comments**: LASCO heights corroborate type III3 source altitudes.
