# Scientific Observation Instrumentation Form

## Summary of the Paper
- **Content Summary**: This study presents a comprehensive, multi‑instrument investigation of the 13 May 2005 solar eruptive event and its interplanetary and terrestrial impacts.  Coordinated observations of the eruption, coronal mass ejection (CME), interplanetary CME (ICME), and magnetic cloud (MC) were obtained from remote‑sensing instruments (white‑light coronagraphs, EUV imagers, X‑ray and radio spectrometers, and interplanetary scintillation arrays) and from near‑Earth in‑situ spacecraft (ACE, Wind, SOHO, Geotail, Cluster, LANL, Double Star).  Ground‑based measurements (magnetometers, auroral imagers, ionospheric photometers) complemented the space‑based data.  The combined data set, spanning 13 May 2005 00:00 UT to 16 May 2005 23:59 UT, enabled the authors to trace the CME’s initiation, propagation through the heliosphere, interaction with the Earth’s magnetosphere, and the ensuing global ionospheric response, providing new insight into CME/ICME dynamics and geo‑effectiveness.

- **Data Collection Period 1: CME front in white‑light**
  - **Time Range**: “The front edge of the 13 May 2005 CME event is observed by the SOHO|LASCO C2 coronagraph at **17:22 UT**, then by the C3 coronagraph at **17:42 UT**.”  
  - **Supporting Quote**: see above.
  - **Wavelength(s)**: “white‑light” (broadband visible continuum).  
  - **Supporting Quote**: Implicit in description of LASCO as a white‑light coronagraph.  
  - **Physical Observable**: “CME front” – bright halo structure in white‑light images.  
  - **Supporting Quote**: “The front edge of the 13 May 2005 CME event is observed by the SOHO|LASCO C2 coronagraph …”  
  - **Additional Comments**: The first C2 appearance occurred at **13 May 2005 17:12:05 UT** and the CME onset at 1 R⊙ at **16:47:34 UT**, indicating rapid low‑corona expansion.

### Wind/Radio and Plasma Waves Experiment (WAVES)
- **General Comments**: Low‑frequency radio receivers (RAD1, RAD2, TNR) monitoring metric–kilometric Type II and Type III bursts to track the CME‑driven shock through the corona and interplanetary medium.
- **Supporting Quote**: “The direct radio observations discussed here were acquired by the **Wind – Radio and Plasma Waves Experiment (Wind|WAVES)** (Ogilvie and Desch, 1997; Bougeret et al., 1995).”  
- **Data Collection Period 1: Type II burst tracking**
  - **Time Range**: “The event was observed at **~17:00 UT on 13 May 2005** at 5 MHz, drifting slowly down to 40 kHz at **02:10 UT on 15 May 2005**.”  
  - **Supporting Quote**: as above.  
  - **Wavelength(s)**: **4 kHz – ≈ 14 MHz** (RAD1, RAD2, TNR coverage).  
  - **Supporting Quote**: “The Wind|WAVES experiment has several sensitive radio receivers: Radio Receiver Band 1 (RAD1), Radio Receiver Band 2 (RAD2), and the Thermal Noise Receiver (TNR); these cover a frequency range from **4 kHz to ∼14 MHz**.”  
  - **Physical Observable**: Dynamic spectra of plasma‑frequency emission from the CME‑driven shock (Type II) and flare‑associated electron beams (Type III).  
  - **Supporting Quote**: “The metric Type II burst was seen in radio emission detected by the Wind|WAVES experiment … The event was observed at ~17:00 UT … drifting … to 40 kHz at 02:10 UT on 15 May.”  
  - **Additional Comments**: Frequency‑drift analysis yielded shock speed estimates (e.g., 1230 km s⁻¹).

- **Supporting Quote**: “Observations of IPS made using the radio arrays of the **Solar‑Terrestrial Environment Laboratory (STELab/STEL), Nagoya University, Japan at 327 MHz** (Kojima and Kak‑inuma, 1987) have been routinely carried out for over 20 years.”  
- **Data Collection Period 1: IPS monitoring of the CME/ICME**
  - **Time Range**: “**13 May 2005 – 14 May 2005** (source J0323+053, 327 MHz) – see Table 5.”
