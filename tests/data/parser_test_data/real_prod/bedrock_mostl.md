# Scientific Observation Instrumentation Form

## Summary of the Paper
- **Content Summary**: This study compares the magnetic cloud (MC) observed near Earth on 20 November 2003 with its solar source—a fast halo CME and an M4 flare on 18 November 2003 originating from active region NOAA 10501. Solar observations from space (SOHO/LASCO, SOHO/MDI, TRACE, RHESSI) and ground‑based Hα imaging (Kanzelhöhe Solar Observatory, Big Bear Solar Observatory, Mauna Loa Solar Observatory) are used to characterize the filament eruption, flare ribbons, and CME evolution, and to compute the reconnection magnetic flux. In‑situ plasma and magnetic field data from the ACE and WIND spacecraft (ACE/MAG, ACE/SWEPAM, WIND/MFI, WIND/SWE) are analyzed with a two‑spacecraft Grad‑Shafranov reconstruction to obtain the MC’s orientation, poloidal and toroidal fluxes. The combined multi‑instrument dataset links the solar eruptive processes to the interplanetary magnetic structure and quantifies flux loss during propagation.
